# Novel-500 #84 and #462 — negative perfect inversion

Both sentences preserve the original finite auxiliary before its overt
subject. A shared declarative rule combines a negatively licensed front,
finite HAVE, its agreeing subject, and a separately licensed past-participle
predicate. It does not make the inverted clause a question.

- #84: the repeated *never* is emphasis, not two negations. The perfect
  *loved Dora* carries both adverbs in the degree comparison. The positive
  standard *as I loved her that night* has its own finite verb and time.
- #462: *nor* negates only the second conjunct. *At least* and *at last*
  frame the affirmative *I was off the sea*. The local prepositional view
  of *off* preserves the original particle entry. In the inverted perfect,
  *thence* marks the origin of *returned* and *empty-handed* describes its
  subject.
