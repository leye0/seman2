# Novel-500 #362 — two causes under one by

The original paragraph contrasts the enjoyment of the landscape with the
memory of the past and the anticipation of the future. The whole clause pair
is one unchanged source utterance. The parser could already prove the active
clause and the passive without its causes. In isolation it could also find a
PP without *both*. But the full *both by* unit did not parse, and an unrestricted
parse of the nominal coordination joined *the anticipation* to *the past*.

The scoped assembly independently proves each finite clause and each nominal
span. It requires an overt *both*, a single *by*, and two nominal proofs joined
by *and*. *And yet* connects the finite clauses, while the second *and* connects
the two causes. The partitive reading is not selected for either *of* group.
*Of the past* stays with *memory*, and *of the future* with *anticipation*.
Both nominals are causes of *embittered*, not of *enjoyed*.

The bounded route accepts the same construction with *but* or *yet* and
with either simple nominals or nominals with separate *of* complements.
Negative controls cover agreement, an agentive passive, a repeated
preposition, and a finite clause in place of the second causal nominal.
There is no change to the general grammar, lexicon, sentence boundaries,
or search limits.
