# Novel-500 #221 and #371 — three finite clauses inside one utterance

Both originals use the same source frame: A; B, and C. Preanalysis
keeps the semicolon, records the two provisional conjuncts on its right,
and preserves the original utterance and corpus boundaries. Grammar
proves their predicates and assembles B and C under the overt coordinator.
A stays outside that coordination and is linked by parataxis.

## #221

The original full parse exhausted its forest budget. The two narrative
clauses on the right are independently finite. On the left, the historical
direct gerund *replying* belongs to passive *was prevented* and is
controlled by Mrs. Bennet. The footman's **entrance** prevents the reply.
The footman carries the note, and *for Miss Bennet* identifies the note's
recipient. The two Bennet references are separate lexical spans. *From
Netherfield* belongs to *came*, while *for an answer* belongs to *waited*.
The discourse pronoun *it* remains overt; this syntax does not claim to
resolve it to the note.

## #371

The original parse reached the deadline. The left-hand clause parses in
isolation without its two adverbs. The scoped rule coordinates *long*
(duration) and *heavily* (manner) on *rang*. The two predicates on the
right are *re-echoed* and *felt*, not *re-echoed* and *surrounded*. The
overt *as if* introduces the hypothetical content of *felt*. In that
scene *hell* is the subject, *all* its quantifier, and *with* governs
both *mockery* and *laughter*.

The scoped assembly changes neither the global grammar and lexicon nor
the frozen cohort and search limits. Independent variants and near
misses check finite agreement, object case, lexical valency, the
hypothetical marker and the two source punctuation boundaries.
