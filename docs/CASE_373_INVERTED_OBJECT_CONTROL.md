# Novel-500 #373 — an object-control question after an assertion

The narrator says that he wishes to soothe his guest, then asks whether
he can counsel such a person to live. The isolated left clause and both
adjective phrases were analyzable. In isolation, the reduced question
could nevertheless rank *can I* as a noun phrase. The full original
had roots but no valid analysis.

The scoped proof keeps the original comma and adversative coordinator.
Only the right conjunct is interrogative, with *can* before *I*. The
base verb *counsel* has a licensed object-control reading. Its object
*one* heads both adjective phrases. The second phrase has local
structure of its own: *of every hope* belongs to *destitute*, while
*of consolation* belongs to *hope*. Neither *of* phrase is partitive.
The same *one* is the understood subject of *live*. The infinitive is
selected by *counsel*, not by an adjective or by *wish*.

Independent variants use different control verbs, modals, pronouns and
adjectives. Near misses test inverted order, subject case, base forms,
adjective complementation and positive object-control evidence. The
canonical parser, original corpus text and search limits are unchanged.
