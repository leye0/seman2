# Novel-500 #319 — a stranded passive and a modal perfect assessment

> Now my desires were complied with, and it would, indeed, have been folly to repent.

Case `84:7ee87a8a9ac10b1f` is a complete Frankenstein utterance. The
preceding narrator describes finally leaving home for university,
providing the context for the fulfilled desires. The source graph
requires no punctuation or boundary repair.

In the first conjunct the finite auxiliary agrees with `my desires`,
and that nominal is the understood complement of the selected stranded
`with` in `complied with`. `Now` is local to this clause. In the second,
anticipatory `it` is not a participant. `Would` selects perfect `have
been`, with nominal attribute `folly` and extraposed subject `to repent`.
The paired commas mark `indeed` as a stance supplement of this evaluation,
not of the passive. The syntax neither asserts repentance nor assigns
its understood subject to `it`.

The independent `agreed to` control has the same two scoped mechanisms
with a different selected preposition and nominal evaluation. Exact
source, lexical selection, agreement, and semantic tests accompany it.
